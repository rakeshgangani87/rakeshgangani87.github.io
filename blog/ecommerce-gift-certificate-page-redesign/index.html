Redesigning a gift certificate page: from a basic form to a guided journey.

Buying a gift certificate is more than filling out a form. I turned a plain purchase form into a structured, visual experience with a live certificate preview and clear guidance on how the certificate works.

Introduction

In eCommerce, even a simple feature like buying a gift certificate benefits from thoughtful user experience design.

Customers need to enter recipient details, choose an amount, personalize the gift, understand the terms, preview the certificate and complete the purchase with confidence.

On this project I improved the Gift Certificates page of an eCommerce storefront, turning a basic form into a more structured, visually engaging and informative shopping experience. The goal was to make the purchase journey clearer while keeping every function customers need.

The challenge: functional but basic

The original page did the essentials. Customers could enter their details, pick an amount and theme, add an optional message and go to checkout.

But the experience was minimal. The form filled most of the page with little guidance around it. There was no preview of the certificate beside the form, so it was hard to picture the final gift, and there was little information about how certificates work after purchase.

Key areas for improvement

  • Weak visual hierarchy: the form appeared without a strong introductory section.
  • Little purchase guidance: customers worked through the form with almost no supporting information.
  • No live preview: the certificate was not shown alongside the form.
  • Basic theme selection: themes were a plain list of radio buttons.
  • Little post-purchase information: delivery, redemption and balance usage could be explained much more clearly.

The goal was not just to modernize the look. It was to build a more guided experience around the existing purchase workflow.

The solution: a guided gift certificate experience

The redesigned page adds a clearer information hierarchy, a two-column purchase layout, a dedicated certificate preview and explanatory content.

1. A stronger introductory section

The page now opens with a prominent Gift Certificates heading and copy explaining what the certificate is for, so customers have context before they start typing.

A dedicated navigation bar keeps the three main actions visible:

  • Purchase Gift Certificate
  • Redeem Gift Certificate
  • Check Gift Certificate Balance

This helps customers tell buying a new certificate apart from managing one they already have.

2. A structured recipient and configuration form

The form is organized into clearly labeled sections, starting with recipient details and configuration. Customers enter:

  • Their own name and email address
  • The recipient's name and email address
  • The gift certificate amount
  • An optional personal message
  • A gift certificate theme

Required fields are marked and related information is grouped, so the form is easier to scan and it's clear what's needed to finish the purchase.

3. A live gift certificate preview

The most important addition is a preview panel beside the form. As customers configure the certificate, the preview shows its amount, selected theme, message and recipient details.

Connecting form inputs to a visible representation of the result reduces uncertainty, and makes the page feel interactive rather than like a conventional form.

4. Visual theme selection

The old vertical list of radio buttons became a visual grid of themes: Birthday, Boy, Celebration, Christmas, General and Girl.

The grid is easier to scan and gives theme selection a more prominent place in the journey. The chosen theme also appears on the preview, linking the customer's choice to the final certificate.

5. Clearer purchase actions

The purchase panel puts the preview, the order total and the Add Gift Certificate to Cart button together, so customers can review their configuration and continue without hunting for the next step.

6. Helpful information on how certificates work

A new section below the form explains three key points:

  • Instant email delivery: the recipient receives the certificate and redemption instructions as soon as the order is complete.
  • Good across the store: the certificate can be redeemed toward eligible products.
  • Use the balance over time: customers don't have to spend the full amount in one order, subject to the certificate's terms.

Answering these common questions next to the purchase saves customers from searching elsewhere.

Before and after: what changed

The redesign changes both the visual presentation and how information is organized.

The key shift is from a form-centric interface to a complete purchase experience.

Why this matters for eCommerce UX

A good product page helps customers answer three questions:

  1. What am I buying? Clear headings and supporting copy establish context.
  2. What will I receive? The preview shows the certificate as it's being configured.
  3. What happens next? Visible purchase actions and delivery information explain the next steps.

When these are answered clearly, customers have a better basis for deciding to buy. That doesn't guarantee higher conversions on its own; real impact has to be measured with analytics, usability testing, checkout completion rates and customer feedback. But the redesign gives a much stronger foundation for that work.

Development considerations

A redesign has to preserve the underlying functionality while improving the interface. For a gift certificate workflow, that means:

  • Keeping correct validation on all required fields.
  • Keeping the preview in sync with every customer selection.
  • Making sure the selected amount and theme carry through to the purchase.
  • Preserving the existing add-to-cart and checkout behavior.
  • Making form controls accessible and usable on small screens.
  • Keeping delivery, expiry and refund information accurate.
  • Testing the full journey, from entering recipient details to adding the certificate to the cart.

The frontend has to work cleanly with the underlying commerce platform, so that a better presentation never compromises the purchase itself.

Measuring the results

The new layout opens up several metrics worth tracking:

  • Gift certificate page engagement
  • Form completion rate
  • Preview interaction rate
  • Add-to-cart rate
  • Gift certificate checkout completion rate
  • Abandonment at individual form steps

For example, analytics could show whether customers interact with the preview, or whether fewer customers abandon the form before adding a certificate to the cart. A controlled comparison or a before-and-after analytics review is needed to establish the actual impact.

Final thoughts

Even a small eCommerce feature benefits from a considered UX approach. A clear introduction, a structured form, visual theme selection, a live preview, prominent purchase actions and helpful supporting content turn the Gift Certificates page from just another form into a guided shopping journey.

The lesson for eCommerce developers: good development is not only about making functionality work, but about making it understandable, usable and easy for customers to navigate.
